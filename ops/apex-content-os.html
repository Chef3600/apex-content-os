Production Pipeline

Order matters here — every stage consumes the output of the one above it. The most common failure in AI production is jumping from a client request straight to generation, which produces pretty footage that sells nothing.

Pre-production

  1. Brief lock FounderIntake complete, claims proofed, assets received. No generation before this is signed off.
  2. Product study FounderLabel geometry, bottle/pack shape, exact color, texture of the food itself. This is what fidelity gets checked against later.
  3. Concept families ClaudeFive strategically distinct angles — not five edits of one. Each gets an objective, a hook, and a CTA before it gets a shot.
  4. Shot list Claude + FounderPer shot: size, lens, aperture, camera move, subject move, key/fill/rim, environment, timing, transition. "Cinematic" is not direction.
  5. Reference buildProduct stills the client supplied become the fidelity anchor. Generate packaging from reference, never from text description.

Production

  1. Stills firstLock the look in stills before spending on motion. Cheap to iterate, and a still that fails will fail worse in motion.
  2. Motion passPour, steam, sear, sizzle, reveal. Food motion is the highest-failure operation in the pipeline — budget regenerations into the estimate.
  3. Continuity checkSame label, same lighting direction, same product state across every shot in a concept family.
  4. RegenerationRe-run failures against the specific defect, not the whole prompt. Log what failed — it becomes the prompt library.
  5. AssemblyCut to the hook. First frame earns the second. Pace to platform, not to taste.

Finishing

  1. SoundFood ASMR carries more than music in this category. Sizzle, pour, crack, scrape.
  2. Type and CTAHook on screen within the first second. CTA legible at thumbnail scale.
  3. Brand lockupClient logo file only — never a generated logo. A regenerated wordmark is a delivery-killer.
  4. Ratio cuts9:16, 4:5, 1:1 per asset. Reframe deliberately; do not center-crop and hope.
  5. QC gate FounderSection 04. Nothing reaches a client without a pass.
Time discipline: log hours per stage from the first paid job. Effective hourly rate is the number that decides whether this business is worth running — and it is the one number no agency owner has until they measure it.

Fidelity & Continuity Gate

Visual quality is not enough. A beautiful generation that changes the client's product, packaging, character or location is a failed generation. Reference fidelity comes before cinematic style — always, permanently, on every asset.

Creative Library

The reusable half of the business. Every job should either draw from this or add to it — that compounding is what turns a 25-hour month into a 12-hour month on the same account.

Concept families — the five angles

Product hero
The product is the subject. Macro, light, texture, no narrative. Sells craft and quality.
Craving
The food in use — the pour, the pull, the bite moment. Sells appetite, not information.
Offer-driven
Promo, bundle, free shipping, seasonal deal. Sells urgency. Shortest to produce, fastest to fatigue.
Origin
Where it comes from, who makes it, why it exists. Sells trust and premium price.
Social-native
Shot like the platform, not like a commercial. Sells because it doesn't look sold. No fabricated endorsements.
Comparison
Against the category norm, never a named competitor. Sells differentiation. Use sparingly.

Hook patterns

  • Sensory open — the sound and texture land before any words do.
  • Contradiction — "the sauce that isn't about heat."
  • Process reveal — start mid-technique, explain nothing.
  • Category callout — name the thing every brand in the aisle gets wrong.
  • Specific number — "90 days in oak," "60 miles," "400 bottles."
  • Use case — "the one you keep next to the stove, not in the cabinet."
  • Objection first — answer the reason they don't buy, in frame one.
  • Scarcity, if true — seasonal, small batch. Only if it is real.

Camera vocabulary

Static
Locked frame. Lets texture and steam do the moving.
Dolly in
Commitment. Ends on the product or the bite.
Orbit
Reveals form and label wrap. Slow, or it reads cheap.
Macro push
Texture at the level of crust, grain, sear, bead.
Overhead
Flat lay, ingredient arrangement, prep sequence.
Focus pull
Moves attention between two objects in one shot.
Tracking
Follows the pour or the plate. Requires stable physics — check twice.
Whip pan
Transition device between concept beats. One per cut, maximum.

CTA patterns

Higgsfield Production Pack

Written before funding so day one is production, not setup. Every rule here exists because it is a known failure mode, not because it sounds professional.

Prompt architecture — eight slots, always in this order

SHOT TYPE + SUBJECT + ACTION + CAMERA + LENS/APERTURE + LIGHTING + ENVIRONMENT + CONSTRAINTS Example: "Macro product shot. A 16oz glass jar of dark roux, blank cream label, on wet dark slate. Jar static, a single droplet running down the shoulder. Camera locked, no move. 100mm macro, f/4, focus on the label plane. Single hard softbox key camera-left, black flag camera-right, deep falloff. Home kitchen counter, near-black background. No text, no letters, no logos, no hands, no people." Never write "cinematic" and expect it to mean anything. It is not direction.

Camera language — the only terms used

  • Static / locked — default. Use unless a move earns itself
  • Slow dolly in — commitment, ends on product
  • Slow dolly out — reveal of context
  • Orbit — form and label wrap. Slow or it reads cheap
  • Macro push — texture at crust/grain/sear level
  • Overhead static — flat lay, prep, arrangement
  • Focus pull — moves attention between two objects
  • Tracking — follows a pour. Needs stable physics, check twice
  • Whip pan — transition only, one per cut maximum
  • Handheld — social-native only, never product hero

Three shot templates — fill and run

PRODUCT HERO "Product advertising photograph. [PRODUCT] on [SURFACE]. [ONE PROP] beside it. Camera static, straight on. 85mm, f/5.6, focus on the label plane. Single softbox key camera-left at 45 degrees, black negative fill camera-right, thin rim light on the [glass/tin] edge, background falling to near black. No text, no letters, no logos, no hands." MACRO TEXTURE "Macro food photograph. [SUBSTANCE] [ACTION — pours / breaks / melts] over [SUBJECT]. Camera static, subject moves. 100mm macro, f/2.8, focus at the contact point. Hard warm key camera-left raking across the surface, steam backlit, dark falloff. No people, no hands, no text." PRODUCT IN USE "Food advertising photograph. Hands in [WARDROBE] [ACTION] with [PRODUCT] over [DISH]. 50mm, f/2.8, focus on the hands. Warm key camera-left, deep shadow right. Hands and forearms only, no face. No text, no logos."

Product reference workflow — the rule that protects the client

  1. Upload client photography firstmedia_upload, then media_confirm. Register as a reference element. Never begin with text-to-image for a product that exists.
  2. Generate from reference, never from descriptionThe packaging shape, color and proportions come from their file. A described jar is a different jar.
  3. Blank the label in generationGenerate with a blank label field. Composite the client's vector in post. This is non-negotiable.
  4. Static camera on any labeled productNo orbit, no dolly, no rotation on a labeled hero. A move makes the composite a tracking job and the text a lottery.

Consistency rules

  • One reference element per recurring object — jar, pan, spoon, hands, location
  • Every prop carries one deliberate flaw — a chip, a scorch, a crack. That flaw is how you verify continuity
  • Key light direction is decided once per location and never changes within a scene
  • Hands only, never faces, unless the client supplies real talent with a release
  • Wardrobe details are written down — sleeve roll count, apron strap, cuff marks
  • Product state ladders — track fill level, doneness, melt and ice across every shot

Lighting and motion standards

  • Single hard key, black negative fill opposite. Deep shadow is the house look
  • Warm 3200K for cooked and finished, cool 5600K for raw and cold
  • Steam always backlit or it reads as smoke
  • Specular highlights on anything wet or glossy
  • Slowest move that works — roughly 3–4cm per second
  • One move per shot. Never dolly and orbit together
  • Physics-heavy shots get start and end frames — interpolate, don't describe the change
  • 4 seconds minimum per generated clip; cut down in the edit

Negative prompt — append to every generation

no text, no letters, no words, no logos, no watermark, no signature, no extra fingers, no malformed hands, no faces, no people in background, no floating objects, no duplicated products, no plastic-looking food, no wax cheese, no smoke instead of steam, no oversaturation, no lens flare, no motion blur on the product, no warped label

Regeneration rules

  1. Three attempts per shot. Hard stop.At attempt four, change the shot. The model is telling you the frame does not work.
  2. Re-run against the specific defectNot the whole prompt. Change one variable and log which one fixed it.
  3. Log every failureDefect, what you changed, whether it worked. That log becomes the prompt library and it is the compounding asset in this business.
  4. Physics failures are structuralBroken pour or gravity means reframe the shot, not more attempts.

Break-even and acquisition cost

  • Cash break-even is trivially low — known fixed costs run $10–30/month. One $750 entry project covers roughly two years of them.
  • The real break-even is time. At one client you are earning ~$43/hour because 30 hours of sales are amortised across a single account. That is the honest number nobody publishes.
  • Five clients is a full-time job at ~147 hrs/month for ~$84/hour. A good job — not yet a business.
  • CAC is measured in hours, not dollars — cold outreach costs time.
  • HYPOTHESIS: ~100 personalised touches to win one client, at ~15 min each = ~25 hours per client acquired. Completely untested. Track it from message one.
  • The number that decides everything: hours per delivered asset. Log it on the first paid job.

Ad Factory

Seven inputs produce five strategically different builds. The differentiation matrix is enforced, not hoped for — if two ads would share a first frame, a protagonist and a pace, the set has failed as a test before a single frame is generated.

Outreach Engine

Fifteen minutes of research produces one true observation. The observation produces the message. Reverse that order and you are writing spam with a name merged into it.

Qualification — all four must pass

  1. Live ads visible in the Meta Ad LibrarySearch the brand at facebook.com/ads/library, filter to their page, All ads. No live ads means no budget and no urgency — skip, regardless of how much you like the product.
  2. Creative is stale or thinSame asset running 4+ weeks, fewer than 4 active creatives, or static-only with no motion. This is the problem you are selling into. If their creative is already strong and varied, they are not your prospect.
  3. Reachable decision makerFounder or a single marketing person, findable by name. If the path runs through an agency of record, the sale is three times longer.
  4. Product is visually producibleA physical product with existing photography. If the product photography does not exist, your first job becomes a shoot you cannot do.

The fifteen-minute research pass

  • Meta Ad Library — how many ads, how old, static or motion, what angles
  • Instagram grid — post cadence, production quality, what gets engagement
  • Website homepage — is there video anywhere, is the hero a photo from 2019
  • Product pages — how many images per SKU, any motion, any lifestyle
  • Recent posts — new SKU, new retail account, an award, a season starting
  • Reviews — the exact words customers use, which is free copy
  • One competitor — what they are doing that this brand is not
  • Press / news — expansion, funding, a festival, a collab
Verification rule: the observation in your first line must be something you personally looked at within the last 48 hours. Never write "your ads have been running since March" unless you saw the date. An observation that turns out to be wrong ends the conversation and earns a screenshot in someone's group chat.
