# The first-client offer

## What the market actually pays

Researched September 2026, US rates:

| Thing | Range |
|---|---|
| Food photographer, half day | $800 - $2,000 |
| Food photographer, full day | $1,200 - $4,000 |
| Studio day **with art direction** | $2,500 - $8,000 |
| Per finished, retouched image | $50 - $375, average ~$125 |
| Full-menu session, 20-30 dishes | $1,200 - $3,500 |

Two things buried in those numbers matter more than the headline:

- **Hidden costs add 40-60%** on top of most quotes - styling, props, usage
  rights. A brand quoted $2,000 often pays closer to $3,000.
- Per-image pricing is usually for **one format**. Reformatting for feed,
  story and paid is extra or does not happen at all, which is why so many
  brands run a 1:1 image cropped badly into a 9:16 slot.

Apex includes both. That is the price justification, and it is a real one.

## The existing entry offer is underpriced

`docs/offers.md` lists the **5-Angle Test at $750** for 5 concepts x 3 ratios
= 15 assets. That is **$50 per asset - the absolute floor of the researched
range**, against a market average of $125.

That price does not read as a bargain to a brand owner. It reads as a
signal that the work is amateur, and it attracts the clients who will be
hardest to keep. Recommendation: retire the $750 tier or reprice it to
$1,500. The kill criteria in `offers.md` already anticipated this - "three
fast yeses means underpriced" - the market data just says so in advance.

## THE PILOT - $1,500

**One product. Ten finished assets. Three formats. Seven business days.**

| Included | Detail |
|---|---|
| 10 finished assets | Hero, texture macro, in-context, negative-space variants |
| 3 formats each | 1:1, 4:5, 9:16 - 30 delivered files |
| 1 revision round | A revision changes an asset; it never adds a concept |
| Full commercial rights | Perpetual, all channels, no expiry, no re-licensing |
| Creative plan up front | Camera, lighting, composition, color, background, hook |

**$150 per asset** against a $125 market average - priced above it, because
three formats and perpetual rights are included rather than billed later.

Compare honestly for the client: a half-day shoot at $800-2,000 yields fewer
finished assets, usually in one format, usually with rights limited or
unstated, plus 40-60% in styling and prop costs on top.

### Terms

- 50% to book, 50% on delivery
- Product shipped to studio, or shot on location if local
- Seven business days from product-in-hand

### Do not discount it

The first client is not buying a discount, they are buying certainty. Cut
the risk instead of the price:

- Half up front means their exposure is $750, not $1,500
- Tight scope, named deliverables, a date
- The creative plan goes out **before** they pay the balance, so they see
  the thinking before the invoice

A discount for "first client" permanently anchors the account low and every
renewal negotiates down from there.

### Founding-client term - the one real concession

If they move to a retainer within 30 days of delivery:

- The $1,500 pilot fee **credits in full against month one**
- Their monthly rate is **locked for 12 months**

This costs margin only when it converts, which is the entire objective. It
rewards the decision Apex wants and cheapens nothing.

## The ladder

| Stage | Offer | Price | Assets | Per asset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pilot** | $1,500 one-time | 10 (x3 formats) | $150 |
| 2 | **Retainer - Starter** | $2,200/mo | 16/mo | $137 |
| 2 | **Retainer - Growth** | $3,600/mo | 30/mo | $120 |
| 3 | **Campaign Build** | $6,500 | 35 + visual system | $186 |

Volume lowers the unit price; strategy raises it. Every tier sits at or above
the market average per asset, and the retainers are where the business
actually is - `offers.md` has the margin math: by month three the brand kit,
approved styles and shot library are built, so the same output takes roughly
half the hours.

**Pilot is the proof. The retainer is the business. The campaign is the
ceiling.** Never pitch stage 3 first.

## Scope guard - say it before they ask

Not included: media buying, ad account management, offer/pricing/landing page
strategy, unlimited revisions.

Never: fabricated testimonials or invented customers, any product, ingredient
or claim that is not real, performance guarantees.

**Apex sells production capacity, not results.** A studio that promises a
conversion lift is either guessing or lying, and both end the relationship at
the first flat month.

## Until there is a paying client

Every portfolio piece stays labeled **CONCEPT / SPEC WORK**. The moment the
first pilot delivers, ask in writing for permission to show it. That written
yes is worth more than the invoice - it is what moves the portfolio off spec
and makes the second sale materially easier.
