# Outreach templates

Rules these obey, every time: one observation the reader can confirm you
actually made. One ask, and it is free. No price in the opener. No link in a
first DM. An easy exit. Never "hope you're doing well." **Never lead with the
technology, and never mention it at all.**

The pitch is better content and more usable assets. Nobody buys a toolchain.

`{{like this}}` must be replaced with something specific. If a slot cannot be
filled with something real, the row is not ready to send.

---

## TRACK A - warm (people Larry already knows)

**This is the highest-conversion list that exists and it needs no
verification tooling.** Chefs, owners, GMs, reps, purveyors, former
colleagues. A warm ask converts many times better than a cold one, and the
first client almost certainly comes from here.

### A1 - the direct ask

> Subject: quick one
>
> {{Name}} - I've started doing commercial content properly, on my own now.
> Product and food photography, short video, the assets brands actually need
> for menus, socials and paid.
>
> You know what my standards are in a kitchen. Same thing, pointed at a
> camera.
>
> Two things I'd value:
>
> 1. If {{their business}} needs product or menu photography, I'd rather do
>    your first one than a stranger's.
> 2. If not - who's the one person you'd introduce me to?
>
> Either answer is useful. No pitch attached.
>
> Larry

### A2 - the referral-only ask (when they are not a buyer)

> Subject: one intro
>
> {{Name}} - not selling you anything. I'm building the content side out
> properly and I'm after one introduction, not ten.
>
> Who do you know running a {{food brand / restaurant / bottled product}} whose
> photography does not match how good the actual product is?
>
> That's the whole ask. Happy to send you the work first if it's easier to
> vouch for.
>
> Larry

**Why this works and cold does not:** they already know the standard. There is
no credibility gap to close, so the email can be short and the ask can be
direct.

## TRACK B - cold

### B1 - day 0, the opener

> Subject: {{your bottle shots / your lunch menu photos / your shelf images}}
>
> {{Name}} -
>
> {{ONE specific thing seen on their site or grid, in the last 48 hours. It
> has to be checkable. "The hero on your shop page is shot on a counter under
> ceiling light" - not "your branding could be stronger."}}
>
> That's a fixable problem and it's the one I'd fix first, because
> {{consequence specific to them - it's the first thing a wholesale buyer
> sees / it's the image doing the most work on the menu / it's the frame
> every ad crops from}}.
>
> I'm a chef. I shoot food and product commercially now. If it's useful I'll
> put together a one-page plan for {{specific product}} - camera, lighting,
> composition, background, and the hook it should carry. Free, no call needed,
> and you can hand it to whoever shoots for you.
>
> Want it?
>
> Larry
> Apex Content Studio

Under 120 words. One observation, one consequence, one free thing, one
question. **No link. No price. No attachment.**

### B2 - day 4, deliver it unasked

Send whether or not they replied. This converts better than the opener,
because it is the only email in their inbox that gave before asking.

> Subject: re: {{same subject}}
>
> Made this anyway - the plan for {{specific product}}.
>
> {{3-5 lines. The actual creative plan: camera position, light direction and
> quality, composition, background, and the one hook the frame should carry.
> Specific enough to be useful to whoever shoots it.}}
>
> Use it with anyone. If you'd rather I just shot it, that's a conversation.
> If not, no reply needed.
>
> Larry

For a prospect scoring 9-10, shoot one frame instead of writing the plan.
Nothing outsells the actual work.

### B3 - day 11, close out

> Subject: re: {{same subject}}
>
> Going to stop bothering you, {{Name}}.
>
> If the photography ever moves up the list, I'm here. The plan I sent is
> yours either way.
>
> Larry

Then genuinely stop. Two follow-ups, then 90 days. A third email converts
nobody and costs the domain.

### B4 - Instagram DM

> {{Name}} - the {{specific product}} shot on your grid is doing a lot of
> work with not much light. I'm a chef who shoots food and product
> commercially. Happy to send a one-page plan for how I'd light it - free,
> no strings. Want it?

No link in the first DM. Ever.

## Reply handlers

### "How much?"

Do not answer with a number alone. Answer with scope, then the number, then
the next step.

> Depends what you need. The usual starting point is one product, ten
> finished assets, delivered in all three formats you'd actually post in, full
> rights, seven business days. That's $1,500.
>
> Most people start there and move to monthly once they see the throughput.
> Want me to put the plan for {{product}} together so you're deciding against
> something real?

### "Send me examples"

> Sent. Worth saying plainly: the portfolio is labeled concept and spec work,
> because it is - those are pieces I produced to a commercial brief rather
> than for a client. I'd rather tell you that than have you assume otherwise.
>
> If you want to see the standard against your own product, I'll shoot one
> frame of {{product}} and you can judge it directly.

**Never imply spec work was client work.** Saying it first is disarming and it
is the thing that survives due diligence.

### "Not right now"

> Understood. I'll leave it.
>
> One thing worth doing whenever you next shoot: {{one genuinely useful,
> specific tip for their product}}. Costs nothing and it's the difference on
> most product shots.
>
> Good luck with {{specific thing}}.

Then set a 90-day reminder and actually honor it.

### "We already have a photographer"

> Good - then you already know what it's worth.
>
> The gap I usually get called for isn't the shoot, it's the volume between
> shoots: the reformats, the seasonal swaps, the fifteen variants a paid
> channel eats in a month. If that's ever the pinch, that's my lane.

---

## What never appears in any of these

- The word AI, or any mention of the toolchain. Apex sells finished work.
- A performance claim. No conversion lifts, no engagement promises.
- An invented client, testimonial, result, or case study.
- Spec work described as client work.
- "Hope you're doing well."
